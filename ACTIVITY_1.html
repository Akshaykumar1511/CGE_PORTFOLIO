☀️ Activity 1: Rooftop Solar PV System (5 kW)

Introduction

Rooftop Solar PV Systems convert sunlight directly into electricity using photovoltaic cells. This 5 kW setup is designed for residential homes, reducing grid dependency and promoting clean, sustainable energy use.

Working Principle

  1. Sunlight strikes photovoltaic (PV) cells in the rooftop panels, exciting electrons in the semiconductor material to create DC (direct current) electricity.
  2. Multiple PV cells are wired into modules and arrays to produce the system’s required DC power output (here sized for ~5 kW peak).
  3. The DC output flows to an inverter which converts DC into grid-compatible AC (alternating current) for household appliances.
  4. A charge controller and battery bank (if present) manage charging: excess solar energy stores as DC for night or outages.
  5. Smart metering monitors generation and consumption; surplus AC can be exported to the grid via a net-metering connection.
  6. A system controller coordinates inverter, battery, and grid interaction to prioritize self-consumption, battery charging, or grid export based on demand.
  7. Periodic monitoring and MPPT (Maximum Power Point Tracking) ensure panels operate at optimal voltage/current for maximum energy yield.

Advantages

  • Reduces electricity bills and carbon footprint.
  • Low maintenance and long lifespan (20–25 years).
  • Government subsidies and incentives available.

Disadvantages

  • High initial setup cost.
  • Dependent on sunlight availability and weather conditions.

Site Selection Criteria

The rooftop should be south-facing with minimal shading and a tilt angle optimized for maximum solar insolation. Adequate structural strength is necessary to support the panels and withstand wind loads.

Applications

Ideal for residential, institutional, and commercial buildings where grid-connected systems can significantly offset electricity consumption.
