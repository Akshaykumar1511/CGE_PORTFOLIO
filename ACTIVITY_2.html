🌤️ Activity 2: Solar Water Heating System (2000 L/day)

Harnessing solar thermal energy for sustainable hot water supply

🔆 Focus

Sustainable utilization of solar thermal energy for daily hot water needs in institutional settings.

🔧 System Overview

Designed a solar water heating system suitable for a university hostel. The setup uses flat-plate collectors, insulated storage tanks, and a circulation system to efficiently deliver 2000 liters of hot water per day, significantly reducing dependency on electric or gas-based heating.

📍 Site Selection

The installation site was chosen based on maximum sun exposure, minimal shading, and accessibility for maintenance.

  • South-facing terrace with 30° tilt for optimal solar incidence.
  • Located near water storage tanks for easy plumbing integration.
  • Free from obstruction by trees or nearby buildings.

Working Principle

  1. Cold water from the main storage tank flows into the solar flat-plate collectors through insulated pipes.
  2. These collectors contain a dark-colored absorber plate that captures solar radiation and converts it into heat energy.
  3. The absorbed heat is transferred to the water flowing through copper tubes attached to the absorber plate.
  4. As the water heats up, it becomes less dense and naturally rises to the top of the insulated storage tank through the thermosyphon effect.
  5. Colder water from the tank simultaneously flows down to the collectors, maintaining continuous circulation without any pump.
  6. The insulated storage tank retains the heated water for later use, minimizing heat loss to the environment.
  7. Hot water is then distributed through the plumbing system for daily use, providing an energy-efficient and sustainable heating solution.

🏗️ System Architecture

The system includes collectors that absorb solar radiation, transferring the heat to water through insulated pipes.

🌿 Advantages

  • Reduces electricity and fuel bills substantially.
  • Low maintenance with long lifespan (15–20 years).
  • Environment-friendly — zero greenhouse gas emissions.
  • Ideal for large-scale institutional and residential applications.

⚠️ Disadvantages

  • Dependent on sunlight availability; performance drops on cloudy days.
  • Higher initial installation cost.
  • Requires adequate roof space for collector panels.
